Character Development with D&D Species

Objective:
In this assignment, you will develop a unique character based on a randomly chosen Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) species. The focus is on deepening your understanding of character creation, focusing on the components of a character’s background and personality using Aristotle’s Poetics. You will explore character traits, physical characteristics, and a backstory, and how those elements can influence a character’s actions, motivations, and decisions in a story.

 

Answer the following prompts and questions. 

  1. Randomly Assigned Species:
    You will be randomly assigned a D&D species (such as Elf, Dwarf, Tiefling, etc.). This will serve as the foundation for your character and will influence your build and backstory. Yyou will not be playing this character in a game. Don't worry about the character stats or nitty gritty details (example: ability checks, AC, and pretty much any numbers you see)

  2. Choose a Class: In DND, a class is more than a job; it's a life calling that can can be influenced by your whole personality, background, style, and character journey!

  3. Character Details (Personality and Traits):

    • Name: Choose a name for your character. Be creative, and think about how your character’s name might reflect their background or culture.
    • Personality Traits: Write about your character’s ethical nature, attitude, and sense of self. Think about how they approach life and their interactions with others. Do they trust easily? Are they brave, cautious, kind, or cunning? List 5 personality traits
    • Allignment: Refer to the allignment page 
    • Strengths and Weaknesses: What is your character good at? What are their flaws or vulnerabilities? These will make them more dynamic. list 3 strengths and 3 weaknesses
      • Example: A strong fighter might be overconfident, or a compassionate healer might be too trusting.
    • Motivations: What does your character want most in life? What are they striving for? How does this tie into their strengths, weaknesses, and personality traits? This will probably tie into your backstory. 1 or 2 sentences for this one.

  4. Physical Characteristics:

    • Appearance: Describe what your character looks like. Focus on key physical features such as:
      • Height, build, and posture.
      • Unique features (e.g., color of skin, eyes, hair, fur, or scales).
      • Distinctive markings, scars, or tattoos.
      • Facial expressions that reflect their personality (e.g., do they smile often or have a serious demeanor?).
    • Body Language: How do they carry themselves? How do they move, stand, or react to different situations? Are they quick and agile or slow and deliberate?

  5. Backstory:

    • Origin Story: Where does your character come from? What is their cultural background and how has it shaped them? Write a complete paragraph for this origin story.
    • Key Life Events: What are some defining moments in their past that influenced who they are today? Think about both positive and negative events that shaped their personality and choices.
      • Example: Were they orphaned and had to fend for themselves? Did they experience a great loss or victory?
    • Conflict/Goal: What does your character want or need? What’s standing in their way? Think of how their backstory can create internal or external conflict that drives them forward in a story.

  6. Character Rendering

    • Visual Representation: You can create a visual representation of your character using drawing, an online tool like Kleki, or HeroForge. Submit this to the seperate assignment "Character Rendering"
      • If you choose to draw your character, focus on illustrating key features you described in your character’s appearance. This helps you visualize them more fully and enhances your understanding of their personality and background.

 

Guidelines for Reflection: As you create your character, think about how they fit into a larger story. Consider how their personality and history will influence their actions and relationships with others. Focus on the following elements from Aristotle’s Poetics to guide your thinking:

  • Character: What makes your character unique, and how does their personality shape their decisions and relationships with others in the story?
  • Motivation: Why does your character do what they do? What drives them forward?
  • Consistency: Is your character’s behavior consistent throughout their story? Does their behavior change, and if so, why?
  • Catharsis: How might your character’s journey evoke an emotional response from an audience? Does their growth, struggle, or failure move people?

Rubric:

  • Character Depth: Come up with personality traits, physical characteristics, and a well-developed and believable backstory. 
  • Creativity: How original and unique is the character design and backstory? Did you think outside the box when developing your character?
  • Visual Representation : Does the character’s visual rendering reflect their personality and backstory in a meaningful way?

Good luck, and remember to have fun exploring the depths of your character’s mind and background! The more creative and detailed you get, the more you’ll understand how to bring compelling characters to life in stories.
